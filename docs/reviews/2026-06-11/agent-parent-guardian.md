# Parent/Guardian Review - 2026-06-11

Reviewer persona: Sandra, 45, mother of a 17-year-old interested in automotive technology. Skeptical of anything that isn't a "real college." Helping pay. Comparing PTC against community colleges and other career schools. Reviewing on a laptop in the evening.

Pages reviewed: programs.html, practical-nursing-clearwater.html, welding-stpete.html, apprenticeships-clearwater.html, adult-education-pathways.html, workforce-continuing-education.html, clearwater-contact.html

Prior issues checked: C1 (program details), H4 (job outcomes/salary), H6 (tuition) from `docs/reviews/issues-tracker.md`.

---

## Trust & Credibility

This is the area where the site has improved the most, and it shows. As a skeptical parent, the first thing I look for is "is this a real, accredited school or a place that takes my money and hands out a worthless certificate." On that count, the redesign mostly reassures me.

- Every page carries a three-logo accreditation block in the footer: "Accredited by the Council on Occupational Education," "Accredited as part of Pinellas County Schools," and "Part of Pinellas County Schools." Seeing that PTC is part of the public county school district (not a private for-profit) is the single biggest trust signal for me. That is the thing that separates this from the diploma mills I'm worried about.
- The nursing page does this especially well. There's a green "trust strip" right under the hero: "Approved by the Florida State Board of Nursing," "Top Ranked LPN Program," and "Clinical partner: HCA Florida Northside Hospital," plus an actual HCA Florida Northside Hospital logo lower down. A named hospital partner is exactly the kind of proof I trust.
- The welding page tells me the "St. Petersburg campus of Pinellas Technical College is an AWS accredited testing facility" and shows the AWS logo. Concrete, verifiable, industry-recognized. Good.
- "Since 1962" in the footer tagline tells me this place has been around 60+ years. That matters to a nervous parent.

What undercuts trust:

- The footer says "&copy; 2026 Pinellas Technical College. All rights reserved" but the COE accreditation block has no link to the actual accreditation report or COE's directory. As the person writing the check, I want to click "Council on Occupational Education" and land on COE's site to verify PTC is listed. Right now the logos are just images. (This matches open issue M8.)
- On welding-stpete.html, the "Top Ranked" style claims are absent (that's fine for welding), but the nursing "Top Ranked LPN Program" badge links to practicalnursing.org, a third-party ranking site. That's better than nothing, but I'd want one sentence saying who ranked it and when.

## Cost & Value

This is my number one practical question and it is only half-answered. I am paying. I need a number, or at least a path to a number, before I'll drive my kid to an info session.

- Both program pages (nursing and welding) have a "Financial Aid & Costs" box that says: "Program length and costs are approximate, and subject to change. Financial assistance is available to those who qualify." That is a non-answer. It tells me nothing about what I will actually pay. Every community college I'm comparing against publishes a per-credit-hour or per-program tuition figure.
- The nursing page does better than welding: it has a real "Program Costs" PDF card linking to a clearwater.myptc.edu resource. That at least gets me to a number. Good. The welding page, by contrast, has two PDF cards that say "PDF coming soon" and link to `href="#"` (they go nowhere). For a paying parent that's a dead end and it reads as unfinished.
- Neither program page shows tuition inline. I have to leave the program page, click "Explore Financial Aid," and land on a separate tuition page to maybe find rates. The nav promises a lot (tuition-aid.html has "Tuition & Fee Rates," "Net Price Calculator," "Scholarships," "Refund Policy") but those weren't in this review set, so I can't confirm they're populated. The fact that a Net Price Calculator is even promised is a strong community-college-grade signal IF it actually works.
- Value vs. a 4-year or community college: the apprenticeship page lands the hardest punch here. "Earn while you learn," "You earn a paycheck while attending classes," and "Complete the program with industry-recognized credentials, real experience, and zero student debt." That "zero student debt" line is genuinely persuasive to a parent funding this. For an automotive-interested kid, the Automotive Electrical/Electronics/Hybrid-EV Systems apprenticeship is exactly the pitch I'd want to hear. This is the page that would make me reconsider my "real college" bias.
- ROI / "is this worth it" messaging is inconsistent. The welding page shows "Avg. Salary $47,540/yr (BLS 2023)" right in the hero stat bar. That is exactly what I want: a real, sourced salary number. But the nursing page has NO salary figure at all, and neither do any of the apprenticeship program teasers. If one program can cite BLS, they all should. The absence on the nursing page is conspicuous and makes me wonder if the number is bad (it isn't; LPN pay is solid, so leaving it off actually hurts them).

## Safety & Campus Life

This is a real gap for me. My kid would be 18 and on a campus I've never seen. I want to know it's safe and supervised.

- The contact page (clearwater-contact.html) has a "Campus Map & Directions" callout that promises "parking information, and accessibility details," and the meta description mentions "parking, and accessibility." But the actual safety/security information (campus security, emergency procedures, who's on site, hours the building is staffed) is not on the contact page itself. It's all deferred to campus-maps.html, which wasn't in this set.
- There are no campus hours anywhere on the contact page. As a parent I want to know "when is someone there if my kid needs help." The "Student Services Hours" link exists in the Current Students dropdown, but that's buried and labeled for current students, not for a prospective parent.
- No mention of a Clery Act / annual security report or campus safety statement on any reviewed page. A community college would have a "Campus Safety" or "Security Report" link in the footer. PTC's footer has "Consumer Information," which may contain it, but it's not labeled in a way a worried parent would recognize.
- Campus life: the nursing and welding "See It In Action" video sections are excellent for this. Real student/instructor videos let me see the facility and the people. That does more to reassure me than any safety paragraph. More programs should have these.

## Outcomes

Mixed, and this is where a skeptical parent decides yes or no.

- Welding outcomes are well done: sourced average salary ($47,540/yr, BLS 2023), AWS certification pathway, and a clear "what you earn" credentials section. I can see the job at the end.
- Nursing outcomes are described qualitatively (prepares for NCLEX-PN, CNA + LPN credentials, "Your Path to RN" articulation agreements with St. Petersburg College) but there is no salary number and, critically, no pass rate or placement rate. For a nursing program, the NCLEX-PN pass rate is THE outcome number parents and students compare across schools. Its absence is glaring. "Top Ranked" is a marketing phrase; a pass rate is a fact.
- No job placement rates appear on any program page. The apprenticeship page implies 100% employment ("employed by a sponsor company from day one") which is great, but the standalone career programs give me no completion or placement data.
- Employer partnerships are shown where they exist (HCA Florida Northside for nursing; AWS testing facility for welding; named apprenticeship sponsors like Bay Area Electrical, IEC, Tampa Bay Machining). Naming real employers is exactly the right move. I'd want this on the automotive pages too.
- This all confirms prior open issue H4 (no job outcomes/salary data sitewide) is only partially addressed: welding now has a sourced salary, but it has not propagated to nursing or the other programs, and placement/pass rates are still missing everywhere.

## Page-by-Page Notes

### programs.html
- What works: Clean A-Z directory, filter by cluster and campus, 40+ programs, every card has a one-line plain-English description. The "Looking for apprenticeships or continuing education?" callout up top routes me well. As a parent browsing for "automotive," I can find Master Automotive Service Technology 1 and 2 (both campuses) and Automotive Collision Technology quickly.
- What needs work: The cards show zero outcome or cost or length info, just a description and "Learn More." When I'm comparing 5 automotive options side by side, I can't tell which is longer, costs more, or pays better without clicking into each one. A community college program finder usually shows length and credential on the card.
- Suggested fix: Add a small length + credential line to each prog-card (e.g., "1,800 hrs · ASE-aligned"). The data already exists on the detail pages; surface a one-line version on the card so parents can comparison-shop without 10 clicks.

### practical-nursing-clearwater.html
- What works: Genuinely strong trust signals (Florida State Board approval, HCA partner, top-ranked badge). Clear 1,350-hour / ~15-month length in the hero stat bar. Real program-cost PDF. Named counselor (Merritt Scott) with email and direct phone extension. Info session details with Zoom link. Articulation to RN. This page would move me toward "yes."
- What needs work: No salary figure (welding has one, this doesn't). No NCLEX-PN pass rate. "Upcoming Classes" shows "TBD / TBD" which reads as either unfinished or "no classes coming," both bad for a paying parent ready to act.
- Suggested fix: Add a sourced LPN salary stat to the hero stat bar to match welding, and add the program's NCLEX-PN pass rate (verbatim from the live source or program office; if not published, log to follow-ups.md for the live owner). Replace "TBD" with the next real start date or "Contact your counselor for the next start date" so it doesn't read as empty.

### welding-stpete.html
- What works: Best outcomes presentation in the set: sourced BLS salary in the hero, AWS accredited testing facility, clear 1,050-hour length, day AND evening schedule, named counselor (Cheri Ashwood) with email and extension, real student video. The "Ready for the next level?" advanced-welding callout shows a career ladder.
- What needs work: The two cost/flyer PDF cards say "PDF coming soon" and link to `href="#"` (dead links). There's also a developer comment in the source flagging an unresolved schedule discrepancy (the official 2026-27 PDF says evening-only M-F 4-9 PM, but the page shows both day and evening). If the advertised schedule is wrong, a parent who plans around it will be furious.
- Suggested fix: Either populate the cost/flyer PDFs or remove the placeholder cards entirely. Empty "coming soon" cards look unfinished and erode trust. Resolve the schedule discrepancy with the counselor before this goes live; do not advertise a day section that may not exist.

### apprenticeships-clearwater.html
- What works: This is the page that overcomes my "real college" skepticism. "Earn while you learn," "earn a paycheck while attending classes," "zero student debt," and the clean 3-step "How Apprenticeships Work." The Automotive Electrical/Electronics/Hybrid-EV Systems card is exactly right for my kid. Named community sponsors add credibility. A real phone number CTA ("(727) 538-7167") to reach admissions.
- What needs work: No earnings range for the apprenticeships ("you earn a paycheck" but how much?). No program length or schedule on the two PTC-sponsored cards. The community programs say "Contact the sponsoring organization to enroll" but give no phone, email, or link, so I literally cannot contact them. That's a dead end for a motivated parent.
- Suggested fix: Add a typical wage range and program length to the two PTC-sponsored apprenticeship cards. For community sponsors, add at least a phone number or website (if none exists on the live site, log to follow-ups.md). "Contact them" with no contact info is worse than not listing them.

### adult-education-pathways.html
- What works: Clear "Choose Your Path" routing for dual enrollment, distance learning, ABE/GED, ESOL, and student orgs, each split by campus. "High school students earn tuition-free PTC credit toward a career program" is a great value line for a parent of a 17-year-old still in high school. Dual enrollment is directly relevant to me.
- What needs work: This page is all routing, no substance. Every card just sends me to another page. For dual enrollment specifically (very relevant to my still-in-high-school son), I'd want a one-line "who qualifies" right here.
- Suggested fix: Add a single eligibility sentence under the Dual Enrollment card (e.g., "Open to current high school students; talk to your school counselor"). Keeps the page useful even before I click through.

### workforce-continuing-education.html
- What works: Honest and clear. The campus-location alert with both phone numbers is helpful. The note that these short courses "do NOT articulate or grant credit toward PTC's program level courses" is exactly the kind of plain honesty that builds my trust. Real registration links (Enrole) and a clean "no sessions currently scheduled" treatment instead of hiding empty courses.
- What needs work: No prices on any course card; I have to click through to Enrole for each. The page title says "Short Courses & Evening Classes" but the nav dropdown that points here is labeled inconsistently ("Workforce & Continuing Education" on one page, "Evening & Part-Time" on another). Minor, but it made me wonder if I'd landed in the right place.
- Suggested fix: If a price exists in Enrole, surface a "from $X" on the card. Standardize the nav label across pages so the destination name matches the link name.

### clearwater-contact.html
- What works: Real address, phone, AND fax. "Schedule a Visit" card explicitly mentions tours and shadow days "before you apply," which is exactly what a cautious parent wants. Class schedule and staff directory links. Clean, professional, easy to scan at night on a laptop.
- What needs work: This is a "Contact & Visit" page with no campus hours, no general admissions email, and no contact form. To reach a real person I either call a main number or hunt through a staff directory. As a parent with a specific question at 9 PM, I want an email or a form I can fire off now. Also, three nav items in the Campus Info dropdown ("Employer Partnerships," "Post a Job for Students," "Advisory Committees") all point to coming-soon.html, which looks unfinished.
- Suggested fix: Add campus/office hours and a general admissions email or a simple "Ask a Question" form to this page. The whole point of a contact page is reaching a human; right now it mostly redirects me elsewhere. Hide or finish the coming-soon nav items before launch.

## Top 3 Issues (ranked by impact on trust and enrollment decision)

1. **No actual cost figure or outcome number on most program pages (confirms C1, H4, H6).** "Costs are approximate" plus a "Financial Aid available" line is not an answer for the person paying. Welding gets it right (sourced BLS salary inline); nursing and the rest do not. Nursing has no salary and no NCLEX-PN pass rate, the single number parents compare nursing programs by. Fix: put a sourced salary stat in every program hero, surface program length + credential on the programs.html cards, and add pass/placement rates where they exist (verbatim from live or routed to follow-ups.md). This is the difference between me requesting info and closing the tab.

2. **Dead and placeholder links read as an unfinished, untrustworthy site.** welding-stpete.html cost/flyer PDFs say "coming soon" and link to `#`; clearwater-contact.html points three Campus Info items to coming-soon.html; the apprenticeship community sponsors say "contact them" with no contact info. A skeptical parent reads broken links as "they don't have it together," which is the exact diploma-mill vibe I'm screening for. Fix: populate or remove every placeholder before launch.

3. **Campus safety and a real way to reach a human are both missing from the contact page.** No campus hours, no security/safety statement, no admissions email or contact form. For a parent sending an 18-year-old to a campus sight-unseen, "is it safe and who do I call" is a gating concern. Fix: add office hours, a general admissions email or inquiry form, and a clearly labeled "Campus Safety" link (Clery/annual security report) findable from the contact page and footer.
