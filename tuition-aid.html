Tuition & Financial Aid

Find PTC's tuition rates, fees, and payment information, along with the financial aid, scholarships, and veterans benefits that can help cover the cost of your program.

Paying Your Tuition & Fees

All students must come to the bookstore to pay fees each term; payment does not happen automatically. Tuition, lab fees, and facility fees are due by the first day of class of each term.

Fees

In addition to tuition, Pinellas Technical College programs and courses are subject to the following fees:

  • Application/processing fee
  • ID card
  • Parking permit
  • Books, tools, and supplies
  • Laboratory fees
  • Equipment and facility usage fees
  • Graduation fees
  • Uniforms (as required by program)
  • Certification or licensure exam assessments (as required by program)

Registration & Fee Payments

Pinellas Technical College tuition and fees are collected upon initial registration and subsequent fee periods. Cash, credit card (Visa and MasterCard), FL Prepaid ID Card, money orders, personal checks, and Cashier's Checks are accepted. Financial aid is available to those who qualify.

  • Fee periods are identified on the website calendar.
  • All students must come to the bookstore to pay fees each term; payment does not happen automatically.
  • Tuition, lab fees, and facility fees are due by the first day of class of each term.
  • If being funded by an outside agency, students must make sure the agency has all necessary paperwork in a timely manner. Pinellas Technical College does not send this information to the agency.
FL Prepaid: Please bring your card to each registration. Currently, only tuition fees are covered. Other fees such as lab, facility, ID, application, etc., should be paid out of pocket or through another type of financial aid.

FAFSA & Financial Aid Eligibility

Financial aid consisting of Federal Pell Grants and various agency scholarships helps eligible students meet educational expenses. For complete eligibility and application details, visit the federal FAFSA site.

Completing the FAFSA

The Free Application for Federal Student Aid (FAFSA) is available at Pinellas Technical College's Financial Aid office or online at fafsa.gov. What you need to fill out the FAFSA:

  • Social Security Number
  • Prior Year Tax Return Documents
  • Driver's License Number
  • Current Bank Statement
  • Mortgage & Investment Records (if any)
  • Alien Registration Number (if not a U.S. Citizen)
  • Parents' Tax Return Documents (if a dependent)
  • Your campus Federal School Code
Each campus has its own Federal School Code. Use 005605 for the Clearwater campus or 013917 for the St. Petersburg campus when completing your FAFSA. Find your campus details on the Clearwater or St. Petersburg tuition page.

In addition, all students must visit Pinellas Technical College's Financial Aid Department to complete an Institutional Application Form prior to starting school. This allows the Financial Aid Department to begin processing financial aid on students' behalf. Without completing an Institutional Application Form in a timely manner, students' financial aid may be delayed.

Verification

Student aid reports may require verification through the U.S. Department of Education or financial aid specialists. Students must provide requested documentation before disbursement, which may include tax returns, social security cards, or selective service registrations.

Satisfactory Academic Progress & Attendance

Students follow qualitative and quantitative institutional guidelines. Minimum Satisfactory Academic Progress and Attendance Requirements exist for each program. Academic progress is calculated as Standard Hours Completed divided by Enrolled Class Hours; attendance is calculated as Hours Attended divided by Enrolled Class Hours.

  • Students must attend 90% of program hours to graduate.
  • All clock-hour program students must meet attendance requirements before graduation.
  • Students on financial aid not meeting 90% attendance have Pell awards prorated to attended hours.
  • At SAP evaluation, students may miss up to 10% per payment period; more requires hour makeup.
  • Pell awards cannot exceed standard program clock hours; dropped hours reduce Pell awards and may delay disbursement.

Return of Title IV Funds

Title IV funds assume full enrollment for the award period. If students withdraw before completing 60.01% of scheduled payment period hours, the institution calculates earned versus disbursed amounts. Excess funds return; unearned aid owed to students may be requested post-withdrawal.

FAME ID Theft Prevention

Pinellas Technical College uses FAME to process Pell Grant awards and disbursements. Visit FAME for information regarding cybersecurity and ID theft prevention.

Federal & State Funding

Most of Pinellas Technical College's financial assistance involves the Federal Pell Grant. Florida residents may also qualify for several state and workforce programs.

Federal Pell Grant

An award to help undergraduates pay for education beyond high school. Students receiving Title IV Federal Assistance are required to have a high school diploma or GED. Eligibility is determined after an application is submitted to the Federal Processing Center.

Florida Bright Futures Scholarship Program

Scholarships based on high school academic achievement. Funding is determined by award level: Florida Academic Scholars (FAS), Florida Medallion Scholars (FMS), or Gold Seal Vocational Scholars (GSV). Submit a Florida Financial Aid Application during your last year in high school.

Florida Public Postsecondary Career Education Student Assistance Grant

Assistance for Florida students enrolled in certificate programs of 450 hours or more. Requires enrollment of at least 180 hours per fee period, a completed FAFSA, and demonstrated unmet need of at least $200, among other state eligibility requirements.

Vocational Rehabilitation

Florida's Vocational Rehabilitation program helps people with disabilities find meaningful careers and may assist with training, medical treatment, accommodations, and other services. Clients must apply in person. Rehabworks

Youth Connect

Assists economically disadvantaged youth ages 16 to 21. The Youth Connect Scholarship funds tuition, books, fees, tools, and uniforms up to $2,500 per student as approved by the Youth Connect Project Manager. See the Youth Connect Coordinator on campus.

Federal Stimulus Job Training Program (CareerSource Pinellas)

CareerSource Pinellas has funding available to assist dislocated workers. General eligibility includes Pinellas County residency, age 18 or older, and U.S. work authorization, among other criteria. CareerSource Pinellas

Scholarships

Below is a list of scholarships available to Pinellas Technical College students. The organization offering the scholarship establishes the eligibility criteria, which is usually based on financial need and/or academic achievement. High school students, contact your high school counselor for additional available scholarships.

The American Welding Society Scholarship

The American Welding Society awards scholarships to students enrolled in a welding-related education or training program. To be eligible, the student must have a high school diploma or GED and attend school in the United States or its territories.

Deadline: March 1 for the following fall term. Apply at the American Welding Society

The Armed Forces Communications and Electronics Association (AFCEA) Scholarship

The AFCEA provides scholarships in fields of study that relate to its sphere of interest. Applications are accepted from students enrolled in Computer Systems, Electronics, Web Design, Telecommunications, or PC support. Requires a minimum 3.0 GPA (unweighted), demonstrated financial need, and U.S. citizenship, among other criteria.

Applications accepted prior to February 28; reviewed in mid-March; awarded in April. Apply at the AFCEA

The Bailey Family Foundation Scholarship

The Bailey Family Foundation awards a $5,000 scholarship each year to a PTC student based on GPA, financial need, and community involvement. The scholarship money may be used for tuition only.

Applications accepted January 1 to March 15 and July 1 to September 15. Apply at the Bailey Family Foundation

Marine Technology Society Scholarship

MTS offers $2,000 scholarships to students enrolled in a two-year technical, engineering, or community college in a marine-related field.

Deadline: April 15 for the following fall term. Apply at the Marine Technology Society

Pinellas Technical College Nursing Service Scholarship

Provides financial assistance for students to pursue the state board examination within thirty to forty-five days upon completion of the Practical Nursing Program. The committee awards two scholarships per graduating class as long as funds are available. Eligibility includes being a Florida resident and a graduate of Pinellas Technical College St. Petersburg, 90% attendance, a GPA of 80% or higher, community volunteer involvement, faculty nomination, and demonstrated financial need.

Application forms are available at the Financial Aid office.

The Passmore Scholarship

Makes awards directly to women, age 21 or over, who live, work, or go to school in the Tampa Bay area, who demonstrate achievement, need, and well-defined educational goals, and who are attending an accredited program. Several awards averaging between $200 and $500 are made each year.

Deadline: April 30. Mail completed forms to BPW/St. Petersburg-Pinellas, P.O. Box 1063, St. Petersburg, FL 33731-1063, or fax to (214) 602-7823. Passmore Scholarship details

Pinellas Education Foundation Scholarship

A variety of scholarships for graduating high school seniors are available through the generosity of local corporations, businesses, agencies, organizations, and individuals. Scholarships vary in amounts and most may be used for vocational education.

December deadline. Apply at the Pinellas Education Foundation

The Barrett Family Foundation VA Scholarship St. Petersburg

The Barrett Family Foundation awards a scholarship to a veteran who is honorably discharged and already qualifies for VA benefits under Chapter 30, 33, or 1606. Recipients must maintain satisfactory academic grades for the program and 80% monthly attendance, and write a thank-you note of appreciation at acceptance and at graduation.

Apply through the St. Petersburg campus

Florida Center for Students with Unique Abilities (FCSUA) / PTC-UP St. Petersburg

PTC-UP is a Postsecondary Transition Program designed for students with intellectual disabilities, providing structured, individualized career pathways leading to certificates of attendance, employability readiness, and potentially industry certifications and licensing. Eligibility includes possession of a high school diploma or equivalent, documentation of intellectual disability, reliable transportation, a commitment to education and employment, and successful completion of an interview process.

Contact: Pinellas Technical College Unique Professionals, 727-893-2500 ext. 2323. Sponsor: FCSUA

Pinellas County Schools maintains an additional, regularly updated list of scholarship opportunities. View PCSB Financial Aid & Scholarships.

How Financial Aid Works at PTC

New to financial aid? Here is the basic process from start to funding.

Apply to PTC

Complete your admissions application and get accepted into a program. You must be enrolled to receive most types of financial aid.

File the FAFSA

Complete the Free Application for Federal Student Aid at fafsa.gov using your campus Federal School Code (Clearwater 005605 or St. Petersburg 013917). This is the first step toward grants and other aid.

Receive Your Award Letter

PTC's financial aid office will notify you of your aid package. Review it carefully and accept the awards you want to use.

Aid is Applied to Your Account

Accepted aid is applied directly to your tuition balance. Any remaining balance is your responsibility; any credit can be refunded to you.

Veterans Benefits

Pinellas Technical College welcomes veterans and their dependents. Most of the full-time programs offered are approved for veterans (VA) educational benefits.

Clearwater Military & Veteran Student Resources
  • Approved for VA educational benefits including the Post-9/11 G.I. Bill® (Ch. 33) and Vocational Rehabilitation & Employment (Ch. 31)
  • Title 38 U.S. Code 3679(e) protections while VA payment to the institution is pending
  • Out-of-state fee waivers for eligible veterans under the Congressman C.W. Bill Young Veteran Tuition Waiver Program

In accordance with Title 38 US Code 3679 subsection (e), this school adopts the following additional provisions for any students using U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs (VA) Post 9/11 G.I. Bill® (Ch. 33) or Vocational Rehabilitation & Employment (Ch. 31) benefits, while payment to the institution is pending from the VA. This school will not:

  • Prevent the student's enrollment;
  • Assess a late penalty fee to the student;
  • Require the student to secure alternative or additional funding;
  • Deny the student access to any resources (access to classes, libraries, or other institutional facilities) available to other students who have satisfied their tuition and fee bills to the institution.

However, to qualify for this provision, such students may be required to produce the VA Certificate of Eligibility (COE) by the first day of class, provide a written request to be certified, and provide additional information needed to properly certify the enrollment as described in other institutional policies.

Chapter 35 VA students will not be certified for any program taught in whole or in part in an online, distance learning, independent study, hybrid, or blended format. All other veterans can attend 100% online, but their benefits will reflect this option.

Veteran's Attendance Policy

Veteran students must maintain a minimum of 80% attendance each calendar month to remain in good standing. Students exceeding 20% absenteeism in a calendar month will have their VA benefits terminated on the last day they were satisfactory for the current month. Students must have 80% attendance the following month in order to be re-instated the first school day of the next month. If a student were to withdraw, the attendance policy remains effective for a calendar year. Minimum attendance standards vary by program; where program standards are greater than 80%, students must adhere to the higher standard. Practical Nursing requires a minimum 94% attendance.

Appeal Process

Students may appeal the denial status of their financial aid by submitting a written request to the Financial Aid Office detailing the student's personal mitigating circumstances. Some circumstances such as medical problems, illness or death in the family, or divorce shall be considered for an appeal. Appeals must be accompanied by documentation from a third party such as a family physician or lawyer. All appeals will be reviewed by the financial aid specialist and an administrator. The student will receive a response to the appeal within one week from the date the financial aid office receives all documentation. If the student finds this decision unsatisfactory, the student may appeal to the campus director. The campus director's decision on the appeal will be final.

Congressman C.W. Bill Young Veteran Tuition Waiver Program

Chapter 2014-1, Laws of Florida, (House Bill 7015) amended section 1009.26, Florida Statutes (F.S.), and created the Congressman C.W. Bill Young Veteran Tuition Waiver Program. This program authorizes an out-of-state fee waiver for honorably discharged veterans of the United States (U.S.) Armed Forces, U.S. Reserve Forces or the National Guard who physically reside in Florida. A career center operated by a school district under s. 1001.44, F.S. or charter technical career center operated under s. 1002.34, F.S., must waive out-of-state fees for an honorably discharged veteran of the U.S. Armed Forces, the U.S. Reserve Forces or the National Guard who physically resides in this state while enrolled in the institution.

Chapter 2014-62, Laws of Florida, (House Bill 851) amended s. 1009.22 and 1009.26, F.S. Career centers operated by school districts and charter technical career centers must provide a tuition waiver for recipients of a Purple Heart or another combat decoration superior in precedence in accordance with the provisions in s. 1009.26(8), F.S.

Refund Policy

Refunds, when due, are made without requiring a request from the student.

  • Active military service. If a student withdraws for active military service, the student is given two options: a full refund or credit with the withdrawal noted for active military duty, or the opportunity to complete the course(s) at a later date without academic or financial penalty.
  • A student may cancel an enrollment at any time before the commencement of a class and shall be entitled to a refund of tuition.
  • Students who withdraw from their career technical programs qualify for a refund of tuition and lab fees. The withdrawal (the last day of attendance) must occur within the first 10 class sessions or first 50 enrollment hours following the entry date on the class registration form, whichever occurs first. Notice of withdrawal must be submitted to the records office.
  • Refunds, when due, are made within 45 days of the last day of attendance if written notification has been provided to the institution by the student, or from the date the institution terminates the student or determines the student has withdrawn.
  • Refunds will be issued for incorrect fee charges, classes that are closed due to low enrollment, or an administrative determination that the student is in the incorrect program.
  • The application fee, facility fee, parking and ID fees are nonrefundable.
  • Tuition paid by Title IV funding (Pell Grant) will be refunded to the grant according to the Title IV policy.
  • Students enrolled in Continuing Workforce Education classes are not entitled to a refund once the class has commenced.
